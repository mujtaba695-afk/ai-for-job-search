# Cover Letter Writer — Performance & Digital Marketing

## Workflow

### 1. Collect Inputs

- **Job description** (URL, text, or file)
- **Resume/experience** (file, text, or verbal description)
- **Company research** (optional — if not provided, research the company)
- **Tone preference** (professional, conversational, bold)

### 2. Analyze & Plan

From the JD, extract:
- Top 3 must-have requirements
- Company culture signals
- Pain points the role solves
- Key metrics/outcomes expected

Map candidate experience to each requirement.

### 3. Structure (400 words max)

```
[Your Name]
[Date]

Dear [Hiring Manager Name / Recruiting Team],

PARAGRAPH 1 — THE HOOK (2-3 sentences)
- Name the exact role and company
- One sentence on why you're excited (specific — not generic)
- Your strongest qualification as it relates to the role

PARAGRAPH 2 — THE PROOF (3-4 sentences)
- Address top 2-3 JD requirements with specific examples
- Use metrics: "Managed $1.5M annual budget across Google Ads and Meta, achieving 4.2x blended ROAS"
- Mirror the JD's exact language

PARAGRAPH 3 — THE BRIDGE (2-3 sentences)
- Address any gaps proactively and positively
- Show you've researched the company (recent campaigns, growth, values)
- Connect your experience to their specific challenges

PARAGRAPH 4 — THE CLOSE (2 sentences)
- Reiterate enthusiasm
- Clear CTA (availability for conversation)
- Thank them

Best regards,
[Your Name]
```

### 4. Special Variants

**Cold Outreach (to hiring manager/recruiter):**
- Shorter (200 words)
- Lead with a specific insight about their company/campaigns
- End with a soft ask (coffee chat, quick call)

**Follow-up Email:**
- Reference application date and role
- Add one new value point not in original letter
- Keep it to 3-4 sentences

### 5. Output

Clean, ready-to-send cover letter. If no hiring manager name is provided, research to find one. If unknown, use "Dear [Company] Marketing Team."

## Rules

- **Never generic** — every sentence should be specific to this role/company
- **No "I am writing to express my interest"** — start strong
- **Show, don't tell** — "I increased ROAS by 40%" > "I am results-driven"
- **Address gaps honestly** — better to acknowledge than to hope they don't notice
- **Match their tone** — startup? Be casual. Enterprise? Be polished.
