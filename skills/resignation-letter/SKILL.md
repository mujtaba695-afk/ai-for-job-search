# Resignation Letter Writer

## Standard Resignation Letter

```
[Your Name]
[Your Address]
[Date]

[Manager's Name]
[Company Name]

Dear [Manager's Name],

I am writing to formally resign from my position as [Job Title] at [Company Name],
effective [Last Working Day — typically 2 weeks from date].

I have accepted a new opportunity that aligns with my career goals in [performance
marketing/paid media]. This was not an easy decision — I've genuinely enjoyed my
time here and am grateful for the opportunities to [specific thing you're grateful for].

During my remaining time, I am committed to ensuring a smooth transition:
- Documenting all campaign structures, processes, and account access
- Training [colleague's name] on [specific responsibilities]
- Completing [any pending projects]
- Creating handover notes for all active campaigns

I wish you and the team continued success. I hope to stay in touch.

Sincerely,
[Your Name]
```

## Resignation Etiquette

| Situation | Notice Period | Approach |
|---|---|---|
| Standard | 2 weeks | Full transition support |
| Senior/Manager | 4 weeks | Comprehensive handover |
| Contract | Per contract terms | Follow contract exactly |
| Toxic environment | Minimum required | Professional but brief |

## What NOT to Do
- Don't badmouth the company or team
- Don't slack off during notice period
- Don't poach clients or colleagues
- Don't post about it on social media
- Don't accept a counteroffer (statistically, most leave within 6 months anyway)

## After Resigning
- Update LinkedIn (new role starts on start date, not when you resign)
- Connect with colleagues you want to keep
- Return all equipment and access
- Don't burn bridges — marketing is a small world

## Rules
- Always be professional, even if leaving a bad situation
- Offer to help with transition
- Keep it brief (one page max)
- Deliver in person first, then follow up with the written letter
